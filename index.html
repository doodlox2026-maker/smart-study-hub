Calculus: Rigorous Limits, Derivative Derivation & Continuity

The derivative f'(x) is formalized via the Cauchy-Weierstrass limit of difference quotients: f'(x) = lim (h → 0) [(f(x + h) - f(x)) / h]. Geometrically, this converts an empirical secant line connecting two points separated by distance h into the unique instantaneous tangent line as h contracts to zero.
Essential Calculus Theorems & Rules
  • Squeeze Theorem: If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g(x) = lim h(x) = L, then lim f(x) = L. Crucial for lim (x→0) [sin(x)/x] = 1.
  • L'Hôpital's Rule: For indeterminate forms 0/0 or ∞/∞, lim [f(x)/g(x)] = lim [f'(x)/g'(x)], provided the derivative limit exists.
  • Chain Rule: d/dx [f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) * g'(x), governing composite functional change.
  • Product & Quotient Rules: (u*v)' = u'v + uv' and (u/v)' = (u'v - uv') / v².
f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x+h) - f(x)]/h | Power: d/dx [xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹ | Tangent: y - y₀ = m(x - x₀)

Matrices: Determinants, Inverses & Vector Spaces

In linear algebra, a matrix M transforms vector space. The determinant det(M) quantifies the precise factor by which basis areas (in 2D) or hypervolumes (in nD) scale under that linear map. A negative determinant signifies an orientation reversal (reflection).
Matrix Properties & Transformations
  • Singular Matrix Condition: det(A) = 0 indicates dimensionality loss; the matrix has no inverse (A⁻¹ does not exist).
  • Matrix Inversion: A⁻¹ = (1 / det(A)) * adj(A), requiring det(A) ≠ 0.
  • Trace Operator: Trace(A) = ∑ a_ii (sum of main diagonal elements), invariant under basis rotation.
  • Cramer's Rule: Solves systems Ax = b using x_i = det(A_i) / det(A).
det [[a, b], [c, d]] = ad - bc | det(A · B) = det(A) · det(B) | det(Iₙ) = 1

Organic Chemistry: Hydrocarbon Hybridization & Mechanisms

Carbon's unique catenation capability stems from orbital hybridization. In methane (CH₄), one 2s and three 2p orbitals hybridize into four identical sp³ orbitals at 109.5°. In ethylene (C₂H₄), sp² hybridization forms a planar σ-framework with one unhybridized 2p orbital creating a π-bond. In acetylene (C₂H₂), sp hybridization forms a linear 180° structure with two orthogonal π-bonds.
Hydrocarbon Classifications & Reactivity
  • Alkanes (CnH2n+2): Saturated hydrocarbons containing only strong σ-bonds; undergo free-radical halogenation.
  • Alkenes (CnH2n): Unsaturated hydrocarbons featuring C=C double bonds; undergo electrophilic addition.
  • Alkynes (CnH2n-2): Terminal alkynes exhibit weak acidity due to high s-character (50%) in sp orbitals.
  • Aromaticity (Hückel's Rule): Planar, cyclic conjugated systems with (4n + 2) π-electrons (e.g. Benzene C₆H₆) exhibit resonance stabilization.
C-C: sp³ (109.5°, 154 pm) | C=C: sp² (120°, 134 pm) | C≡C: sp (180°, 120 pm)
Figure 2.1: Ethyne molecular geometry featuring a central σ-bond and two orthogonal π-clouds.

Periodic Table & Bonding: Energetics, Lattices & Dipoles

Chemical bonds form because the bound configuration minimizes the system's potential energy. Electronegativity differential (ΔEN) dictates bond character: ΔEN > 2.0 produces ionic bonds via complete electron transfer, 0.4 < ΔEN < 2.0 produces polar covalent bonds, and ΔEN < 0.4 yields non-polar covalent bonds.
Periodic Trends & Intermolecular Forces
  • Effective Nuclear Charge (Z_eff): Increases across periods, drawing valence shells closer and boosting electronegativity.
  • Born-Haber Cycle & Lattice Energy: U = (N_A * M * z⁺ * z⁻ * e²) / (4πε₀r₀) * (1 - 1/n), calculating ionic crystal strength.
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Strong directional dipole attraction between H bonded to N, O, or F and a nearby lone pair (e.g. H₂O boiling elevation).
  • Metallic Delocalization: Electron sea model explains thermal/electrical conductivity and malleability.
Coulomb: F = k(q₁q₂)/r² | Octet Rule: ns²np⁶ | Dipole Moment: μ = q × d (Debyes)

Classical Mechanics: Kinematic Calculus, Vectors & Free Fall

Kinematics describes motion without regard to causing forces. By treating acceleration a(t) = dv/dt = d²s/dt² as a constant vector, double integration yields Galileo's kinematic equations: v(t) = u + at and s(t) = ut + (1/2)at². Eliminating parameter t produces the work-energy invariant v² = u² + 2as.
Kinematic Principles & Analysis
  • Graphical Calculus: Slope of s-t graph is velocity v; slope of v-t graph is acceleration a; area under v-t graph is displacement s = ∫ v dt.
  • Free Fall Acceleration: In vacuum, all bodies accelerate downward at g ≈ 9.8 m/s² regardless of inertial mass, validating Galileo's equivalence.
  • Projectile Motion: Motion decomposes into independent orthogonal components: horizontal uniform velocity (ax = 0) and vertical free fall (ay = -g).
v = u + at | s = ut + ½at² | v² = u² + 2as | s = ∫ v(t) dt
Figure 3.1: Velocity-Time curve illustrating acceleration slope and displacement area.

Electromagnetism: Faraday, Lenz's Law & Maxwell Dynamics

Magnetic flux Φ_B = ∫ B · dA = B A cos θ represents the surface integral of magnetic B-field lines passing through a closed loop. Faraday's Law states that any time variation in flux induces an electromotive force (EMF). Lenz's Law introduces the negative sign: EMF = -dΦ_B/dt, ensuring energy conservation.
Electromagnetic Applications & Phenomena
  • Lenz's Energy Conservation: Induced currents generate an internal magnetic field counteracting flux change; without opposition, perpetual energy generation would occur.
  • Mutual Induction & Transformers: Alternating primary flux induces EMF in secondary coil: Vs / Vp = Ns / Np.
  • Eddy Currents (Foucault Currents): Closed swirling current loops induced within bulk conductors causing Joule heating (I²R) and magnetic braking.
EMF = -N (dΦ / dt) | Φ_B = B · A cos(θ) | Mutual: EMF₂ = -M (dI₁ / dt)
Figure 3.2: Solenoid magnetic induction opposing magnetic flux ingress via Lenz's Law.

Grammar & Syntax: Structural Clauses & Syntactic Trees

English syntax organizes meaning hierarchically through clauses. An independent clause contains a subject and predicate that forms a complete semantic thought. A subordinate (dependent) clause begins with a subordinating conjunction (although, because, since, while) and modifies the main clause.
Syntactic Mechanics & Tense Consistency
  • Comma Splices & Run-ons: Joining two independent clauses with only a comma creates an error; correct using a semicolon (;) or coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS).
  • Past Perfect Tense: Formed with had + past participle to signal an action completed prior to another past event.
  • Voice Transformation: Active voice (Agent + Verb + Patient) provides clarity over passive voice (Patient + Auxiliary 'to be' + Verb).
  • Dangling Modifiers: Modifying phrases must immediately precede the nominal agent performing the action.
[Subordinate Clause + Comma] + [Independent Clause] = Complex Sentence Architecture

Poetics & Criticism: Scansion, Conceits & Shakespearean Sonnet

Poetic analysis interprets rhythm, acoustic phonetics, and metaphorical conceits. English verse predominantly utilizes Iambic Pentameter—five metrical feet per line consisting of an unstressed syllable followed by a stressed syllable (da-DUM: ˘ /), mirroring natural human heartbeat and English speech cadences.
Poetic Devices & Critical Frameworks
  • The Extended Conceit: A prolonged, elaborate metaphorical comparison unifying an entire poem (e.g. Shakespeare comparing his beloved to a summer's day in Sonnet 18).
  • The Sonnet Volta: The rhetorical turn in thought or argument occurring typically at Line 9 (Petrarchan) or Line 13 couplet (Shakespearean).
  • Acoustic Devices: Alliteration (initial consonant sounds), Assonance (internal vowels), and Consonance reinforce emotional timbre.
  • Personification: Endowing non-human entities with conscious volition and human feelings.
Meter: (da-DUM) × 5 = 10 Syllables | Rhyme: ABAB CDCD EFEF GG | Form: 3 Quatrains + 1 Couplet
